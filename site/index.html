Liquid glass for the icons you already have.

glassity reads the outline of any inline SVG and draws it as glass that bends the page behind it. Lucide, Phosphor, Heroicons or your own file. Add data-glass and you are done.

npm i glassity

Glass bends. It does not blur.

Lines that cross the edge of a lens break and reappear a little to one side, the way a straw seems to snap at the surface of water. That shift is the whole trick.

The icons over this text are ordinary SVG. Behind them is ordinary HTML: paragraphs, coloured blocks, a highlighted phrase. glassity takes a picture of it and looks through it.

Short strokes become round tubes of glass. Wide shapes get a flat top and a bevelled rim. Both come from the same outline, untouched.

When this text changes, the picture is taken again.

In a real app

The same glass inside real interfaces: a dashboard built from the shadcn/ui block dashboard-01, a mobile app screen, a landing page, and three templates shared by the v0 community (credited under each). Everything around the glass is ordinary React and Tailwind; the glass is one <GlassProvider> and data-glass on the icons.

Get started

Do it yourself

npm i glassity

Then add data-glass to an icon and turn glassity on once. The code panel in the playground above writes the exact setup for React, Vue, Svelte, HTML or plain JavaScript, with your settings filled in. Next.js, Nuxt, SvelteKit, Astro, Angular and Remix are in the README.

It bends what is behind it.

Light is refracted through the icon by Snell's law, with optical depth, colour split at the rim, frost, a moving highlight and a soft shadow. Over a wallpaper, a photo, a video or plain HTML.

Your SVG stays.

The original icon is kept in the page, so its label, focus and clicks keep working. Without WebGL2 it simply shows as before.

Any framework.

React, Vue, Svelte, Solid, Preact, Angular, Remix, or a script tag. Server rendering is fine.
